# Subsidiary Concentration × Audit-Evidence Escalation Edge

Status: `SHADOW_RESEARCH`
Priority: `MEDIUM`
Production use: `PROHIBITED_UNTIL_VALIDATED`
Last updated: 2026-08-02 JST

## Research question

When a listed parent depends heavily on one subsidiary for revenue, and that subsidiary cannot provide sufficient evidence or explanations to the auditor, does the combination predict a materially higher probability of:

- disclaimer / inability to conclude,
- filing delay,
- special-attention designation,
- financing or covenant pressure,
- further negative abnormal returns beyond the first disclosure?

This is not a generic subsidiary-misconduct signal. The proposed interaction is:

`subsidiary economic concentration × audit-evidence failure × weak parent oversight`.

## Initial primary-source seed

### REVOLUTION (8894)

JPX designated REVOLUTION as a Special Attention Security effective 2026-07-25. JPX stated that the review conclusion could not be expressed because accounting at subsidiary Yamawake could not be supported with audit-required materials and explanations. JPX also stated that Yamawake accounted for approximately 90% of consolidated sales and that the listed parent did not recognize or correct the subsidiary-management risk in time.

The case is useful because the subsidiary was not peripheral: audit-evidence failure affected the economic core of the group.

## Candidate mechanism

1. Investors often initially classify a problem as isolated to a subsidiary.
2. If the subsidiary represents a dominant share of revenue, separability is economically false even when legal entities are separate.
3. Failure to provide audit evidence is an information-production failure, not merely a one-off accounting adjustment.
4. Parent oversight weakness raises the probability that additional transactions, valuations, liabilities, or related-party issues remain unresolved.
5. The market may underprice the interaction because headline classification focuses on the subsidiary event rather than group dependence.

## Proposed features

For each case, record point-in-time:

- subsidiary revenue share,
- subsidiary operating-profit / EBITDA share,
- asset and cash-flow contribution,
- customer and financing concentration,
- whether audit evidence was unavailable, inconsistent, delayed, or refused,
- whether management explanations were unavailable or contradicted,
- audit opinion / review conclusion state,
- filing deadline extensions,
- parent board and internal-audit oversight findings,
- auditor change or resignation,
- special-attention / improvement-report / penalty stages,
- refinancing, covenant, going-concern, and equity-issuance needs,
- D0, D+1, D+3, D+5, D+20 abnormal returns,
- liquidity, spread, borrow availability, and short cost.

## Interaction hypotheses

### H1: Concentration threshold

Audit-evidence failure has larger negative continuation when the affected subsidiary contributes at least 50% of consolidated revenue or operating cash flow.

### H2: Non-separability

Cases described as a subsidiary issue but with high economic concentration should be scored as high Corporate Contagion Risk and low Separability Index.

### H3: Financing amplifier

The effect should be strongest where the parent needs refinancing, has covenant sensitivity, weak cash generation, or likely equity issuance.

### H4: Resolution asymmetry

A later clean audit conclusion or complete evidence restoration may produce stronger positive uncertainty-resolution returns than a generic remediation announcement.

## Confounders

Control or exclude:

- concurrent earnings or guidance revisions,
- capital raises, warrants, or dilution,
- asset sales and M&A announcements,
- market-segment or delisting actions already known,
- microcap liquidity and price-limit effects,
- crypto / real-estate / fund-valuation beta,
- macro or sector shocks,
- prior momentum and distress.

## Falsification

Reject or downgrade if:

- subsidiary concentration adds no explanatory power beyond generic disclaimer-of-opinion events,
- abnormal returns disappear after liquidity and financing controls,
- only one highly speculative microcap drives the effect,
- the necessary concentration data were unavailable at the decision checkpoint,
- entry is not executable after the official disclosure timestamp,
- borrow and gap costs eliminate net alpha,
- untouched holdout fails.

## Promotion gate

Do not promote unless:

- there are enough independent Japanese listed-company cases across sectors,
- concentration data are point-in-time reproducible,
- the interaction adds information beyond audit-opinion severity and generic distress,
- realistic execution and short costs leave positive net alpha,
- no single issuer dominates PnL,
- untouched holdout passes,
- the feature improves Separability and Corporate Contagion calibration.

## Current assessment

`RESEARCH CANDIDATE`, not a trading signal.

The practical advance is a clearer hard distinction between legally isolated subsidiary misconduct and economically non-separable subsidiary failure. The next task is to backfill disclaimer / conclusion-unavailable cases and join them to subsidiary contribution data available before each event.
